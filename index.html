DATA ENGINEERING

Green Grass Library Data Warehouse

Our team built a MySQL data warehouse to analyse completed library loans.

My contribution

I standardised dates and status values, corrected a membership tier reference, kept 43 missing occupations as NULL, and removed one loan row whose staff, book copy, and dates could not be recovered. After loading 12,344 operational loans, I checked for invalid references and due dates. All four checks returned zero exceptions.

Warehouse and analysis

Our star schema stored one row per completed loan of a physical copy, with member, staff, book, and time dimensions. The team wrote eight SQL analyses. One found that nearly 70% of completed loans started on weekends. Another compared loans per copy across genres to account for collection size.

RippleFix.

RippleFix explores how to restore a disrupted timetable using validated room constraints, explainable recommendations, and explicit human approval.

The challenge

A replacement must meet capacity and equipment requirements, avoid timetable conflicts, and respect unavailable rooms.

Technical approach

Deterministic scoring and cascading recovery underpin an agent-assisted prototype. The team designed read-only investigation tools, review gates, and safeguarded Calendar/Gmail adapters; live LLM/provider execution remains unverified.

PROJECT BRIEF / 002
COMPUTER VISION / NATURAL LANGUAGE

Handwriting and Sentiment Classification.

Comparing how neural architectures process two different inputs: handwritten characters and written reviews.

Computer vision

The selected CNN reached 95.34% test accuracy on EMNIST letters without augmentation. Similar letters such as I/L and G/Q remained difficult to distinguish.

Language

I compared SimpleRNN, LSTM, GRU, and bidirectional LSTM architectures on English and Malay pizza reviews. The selected bidirectional LSTM reached 86.44% test accuracy, with neutral reviews the hardest to classify.
